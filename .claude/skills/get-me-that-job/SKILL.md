# Get me that job

Make a focused, realistic plan to win one specific job. Every answer and talking point comes from the user's real experience in the master CV, not generic advice.

Works for any profession. Take the field from the master CV and the ad, and when unsure how hiring works in that field, ask the user.

## Inputs

1. **Master CV** (`master-cv.md`). If missing, suggest `grill-me-for-my-cv` first.
2. **The job ad**, and the tailored CV if `gen-me-a-cv` was already run for it.
3. **Ask the user** (one short round, skip anything already known):
   - Where are you in the process? (not applied, applied, interview booked)
   - Interview date and format if known (phone screen, panel, practical test, presentation, assessment centre)?
   - How many hours a day can you put into prep?
   - Anything you already know about how they hire, or worries about this one?

## Step 1: Research the employer

Search the web for current information. Do not rely on memory for anything that changes.

- What the organisation does, who it serves, and its size.
- Recent news (last 6 months): growth, changes, launches, closures, leadership changes.
- How they work, where findable: tools, systems, methods, standards or regulations that matter in this field.
- Culture and hiring process signals (careers page, employee reviews; treat these as rough hints).
- Who the user might meet: hiring manager or team lead, if the ad or LinkedIn makes it findable. Only publicly available professional information.

Keep this section short and cite sources.

## Step 2: Match and gaps

- Top 3 selling points: where the user's experience most clearly fits this role, each tied to a STAR story from the master CV.
- Gaps: missing or rusty skills from the ad, ranked by how likely they are to come up.
- For each gap, the fastest honest fix: something to revise, a short course, a small piece of work to show, or a way to talk about related experience.

## Step 3: Interview prep

- **Their questions:** 8 to 12 likely questions mixing behavioural, role-specific and practical. For each, a short answer outline using the user's own stories and numbers. Bullet points to speak from, not a script.
- **Practical or skills assessment:** work out what this field usually tests (for example a coding test, case study, trade test, teaching demo, cooking trial, role play, presentation, portfolio review, numeracy or psychometric test). Ask the user if unclear. Give the likely topics and 2 to 3 practice tasks for each.
- **The awkward ones:** prepare honest, confident answers for whatever applies: gaps in employment, retrenchment or dismissal, career change, salary expectations, "why are you leaving", being over- or under-qualified. Keep them brief and forward-looking.
- **Their questions for them:** 5 smart questions specific to this employer and role.
- **Salary:** if the ad doesn't state a band, look up current market ranges for the role and location and suggest how to answer. Present them as ranges with sources, not advice.

## Step 4: The plan

A day-by-day schedule from today to the interview date (or a 7-day plan if no date). Fit it to the hours the user said they have. Each day gets 2 to 4 concrete tasks, for example "Practise answers 1 to 4 out loud" or "Do one timed practice of the assessment". The last day is light: review, logistics (route, outfit, documents to bring) and rest.

## Deliver

- Make it a document the user can keep and come back to, named `Prep - {Company} - {Role}`.
- Offer to run a mock interview right away, asking the questions one at a time and giving feedback after each answer.
- After the interview, offer to log what was asked and update the master CV with anything new.
